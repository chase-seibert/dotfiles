# App Naming

Use this guidance when naming or renaming Chase's iOS and macOS apps, checking
whether a candidate appears available, or developing a portfolio-level naming
system.

## Naming Objective

Names should be memorable, easy to say and spell, searchable, and distinctive
enough to support a durable brand. They do not need to explain every feature.
Use the App Store subtitle, icon, screenshots, and description to establish the
category and core value.

Prefer a strong standalone product name over perfect portfolio consistency. A
shared naming system should make good names easier to generate, not force a
weaker name onto an app that already has a good one.

## Chase-Based Portfolio Strategy

Chase is useful as a portfolio element because it is short, relatively uncommon
as a first name, and also a verb. The preferred construction is a one-word,
CamelCase compound with `Chase` as the suffix:

- `LiftChase`
- `MacroChase`
- `HighlightChase`

Choose a first word that represents something the customer genuinely pursues.
This makes the name feel intentional rather than mechanically assembled. Reps,
macros, progress, moments, and highlights work better in this construction than
broad category labels such as nutrition or strength.

Use the compound spelling in product and App Store presentation while allowing
people to pronounce it as two words. The compound is typically easier to search,
differentiate, and use as a domain or handle.

Avoid these patterns by default:

- `Chaser` as the shared element. It is crowded and has alcohol, nightlife,
  dating, and pursuit connotations.
- Leading with Chase, as in `Chase Strength`. It can sound like a command or a
  personal coaching service and competes with the enormous JPMorgan Chase search
  footprint.
- Adding Chase to another product's distinctive name. The compound must be
  independently defensible; a suffix does not cure confusing similarity.
- Forcing Chase into an app whose existing standalone name is clearly better.

Do not imitate JPMorgan Chase trade dress. In particular, avoid treating Chase
as a standalone house mark alongside a blue octagon or finance-like visual
language.

## Current Portfolio Direction

These are current recommendations, not permanent availability conclusions:

| Product | Preferred name | Suggested App Store subtitle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared strength-training log | **LiftChase** | *Shared strength workout log* |
| Focused calorie and nutrition tracker | **MacroChase** | *Focused calorie & macro log* |
| Rolling-buffer sports camera | **Sideline Save** | *Save the play after it happens* |

Keep **Sideline Save** while it remains usable. It is descriptive, memorable,
alliterative, and already has a public App Store listing. Connect it to the
portfolio through language such as **A Chase app** or **Made by Chase** on the
website, onboarding, and About screen rather than weakening the product name.

If strict Chase-name consistency becomes more important than preserving
Sideline Save, the preferred replacement is **HighlightChase**. **MomentChase**
is warmer but less descriptive, and **SidelineChase** preserves the context but
does not communicate the save-after-the-play benefit as clearly.

Standalone names may still outperform the system for an individual product.
For example, **Setmates** emphasizes the strength app's unusual shared-workout
behavior, while **Dialed** expresses the nutrition app's selective-focus model.
Compare those product-specific advantages with the portfolio value of
LiftChase and MacroChase before finalizing a rename.

## Candidate Evaluation

Score serious candidates on:

1. **Product fit:** Does the name evoke the core benefit or emotional outcome?
2. **Clarity:** Can a prospective customer form a useful first impression?
3. **Memorability:** Is it easy to recall after hearing it once?
4. **Speakability:** Does it work in “Open ___,” “I logged it in ___,” and
   “Download ___”?
5. **Searchability:** Can users find it without fighting generic results or a
   dominant unrelated brand?
6. **Extensibility:** Will the name survive plausible product expansion?
7. **Portfolio fit:** Does it feel related to Chase's other apps without looking
   cloned or formulaic?
8. **Ownability:** Are there confusingly similar products or marks in related
   categories?

Watch for semantic reversals. For example, **CalorieChase** can sound like the
user wants to consume more calories, which is useful for bulking but contrary to
many customers' expectations. **MacroChase** better describes pursuing chosen
targets and allows the product to cover both calorie and macronutrient goals.

## Availability and Clearance Workflow

Availability is time-sensitive. Browse current sources every time rather than
relying on an earlier brainstorm.

1. Search the public U.S. App Store and relevant international storefronts for
   the exact name, spacing variants, phonetic variants, and close compounds.
2. Search Google Play even if the initial product is Apple-only.
3. Search the broader web for software, services, companies, creator handles,
   and unreleased-product landing pages.
4. Check `.com` and any intended alternate domains through registry data and a
   registrar. A missing registry record is only a momentary availability signal.
5. Search social handles when social acquisition is part of the plan.
6. Search USPTO records for exact and confusingly similar marks used with
   software, fitness, nutrition, photography, video, or other related services.
7. Attempt to create the app record in App Store Connect. Public search cannot
   reveal another developer's unpublished name reservation.
8. Reserve promising App Store names and domains promptly when authorized.
9. Before investing materially in branding, obtain a comprehensive trademark
   clearance or advice from a trademark attorney.
10. Record the screening date, storefronts, queries, and meaningful conflicts in
    the relevant project's naming document.

Do not describe a candidate as definitively “available,” “safe,” or “not taken”
based only on public searches. Use language such as **preliminary green signal**,
**no exact public match found**, or **requires formal clearance**.

## Legal Guardrails

- Product names and short phrases are generally not protected by copyright;
  trademark and unfair-competition law are the relevant concerns.
- Popularity is not the test. A small existing product may have trademark or
  common-law rights.
- The whole mark, similarity in sound and appearance, the relationship between
  the goods or services, and likely consumer confusion matter.
- A descriptive or generic first word is generally a better Chase compound
  ingredient than another company's distinctive brand.
- App Store acceptance, domain registration, and trademark clearance are
  separate questions. Passing one does not guarantee the others.

## Expected Naming Deliverable

When helping with an app name, provide:

- One clear recommendation and the reasoning behind it.
- Two to four credible alternatives with explicit tradeoffs.
- A proposed App Store name, subtitle, and short spoken-positioning line.
- A dated availability table distinguishing public-store, web, domain, and
  trademark signals.
- Specific conflicts that eliminate otherwise attractive candidates.
- A reminder that App Store reservation and formal trademark clearance remain
  separate final steps.
